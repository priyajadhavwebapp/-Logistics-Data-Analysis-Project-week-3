This report presents an end-to-end exploratory data analysis and visualization of a hypothetical logistics operation using Python. A simulated dataset of 300 shipments was created with fields covering shipment date, region, transport mode, shipment type, distance, volume, weight, promised and actual delivery duration, and transportation cost in Indian rupees. The report explains the dataset structure and analysis methodology, checks missing values and duplicate shipment identifiers, and documents the cleaning approach used for numeric and categorical fields. It calculates descriptive statistics including mean, median, range, and quartiles, and examines correlations among distance, shipment size, delivery time, delay, and cost.

Seven visualizations are included: a delivery-time histogram, average cost by transport mode, delivery-time boxplot by region, distance-versus-cost scatter plot, numeric correlation matrix, on-time delivery rate by transport mode, and a seven-day rolling average of daily transportation cost. Each chart is justified and interpreted in terms of operational efficiency, cost drivers, service reliability, and potential bottlenecks. The findings are translated into practical recommendations, including regular KPI monitoring, investigation of late shipments by route and mode, balanced evaluation of cost and reliability, and improved data-quality controls.

The project also includes a reproducible Python analysis script, the simulated raw dataset, cleaned data, CSV summary tables, and an interactive Streamlit dashboard with filters for region, transport mode, and shipment type. The dashboard displays key performance indicators and interactive Plotly charts, and allows filtered data to be downloaded. All records are hypothetical and intended for academic demonstration; conclusions should be validated using real operational data before being used for business decisions.
